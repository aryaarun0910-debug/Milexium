# Open R&D questions

Written for a second opinion (Codex or any outside technical reviewer) on the current state of the model/data work. These are genuine open calls, not solved problems dressed up as questions — where I made a judgment call under time pressure, I've said what I chose and why, so a reviewer can push back on the actual reasoning rather than re-deriving context. See knowledge/docs/JOURNAL.md for the full narrative and knowledge/docs/DATA_SOURCES.md for what data backs each claim.

## Model architecture

1. **Is pretrain-then-finetune the right transfer strategy here, or is it overkill?** _legacy/kaggle/transitpulse_transformer.ipynb pretrains one transformer on ~2B real mass-transit events (NYC TLC, Chicago TNP, MTA bus/subway) then fine-tunes on AAR paratransit demand (~93k rows) with the backbone at 5% learning rate and fresh embeddings at full rate. The alternative I rejected was a single joint-loss model across all datasets (risk: the huge non-paratransit volume drowns the actual target signal in every gradient step) and the alternative I didn't build was parameter-efficient adapters (LoRA-style) instead of full-backbone fine-tuning at reduced LR — would that transfer better with less risk of catastrophic forgetting, given the backbone was never trained toward AAR's task at all?
2. **Are dataset+zone compositional embeddings the right way to unify heterogeneous entity vocabularies** (TLC's 265 zones, Chicago's 77 areas, MTA's ~300 bus routes and ~500 subway stations, AAR's 5 boroughs), or would a learned zone-embedding-from-features approach (e.g., embed each entity from its own static attributes — population density, land use — rather than a lookup table) generalize better to a brand-new agency's zones that the model has never seen at inference time? The current design has no zero-shot story for a new city; every new deployment needs its own fine-tune pass.
3. **XGBoost vs the transformer for the actual production model:** realdata/train_real_models.py's XGBoost (count:poisson, lag features, 90-day holdout) already gets R2 0.987 vs 0.923 naive on AAR. The transformer notebook is explicitly told to compare against this and not claim victory by default. Is a transformer even the right tool for a ~5-borough, hourly, single-city target with this little data, or is this pretrain/finetune exercise mainly valuable as an R&D/credibility artifact rather than something that should ship as the production model?
4. **Conformal conditioning came back null.** realdata/train_real_models.py's conformal_two_way tested hour-of-day-bucket conditioning against plain prediction-decile conditioning and got no improvement (97.8% vs 98.1% coverage, near-identical width) — both are also more conservative than the 90% target, meaning the intervals are wider than they need to be. Is there a better conditioning variable (weather regime, holiday flag, a zone cluster id) that would actually tighten these, or is the overcoverage really just distribution shift between the calibration window (partly winter) and the validation window (spring), in which case the fix is a better calibration-window choice, not a better conditioning variable?

## Data engineering

5. **Deadhead cost attribution is uniform-hours based** (business/ntd/build_economics.py: `deadhead_cost = opex * deadhead_hours / actual_hours`), which assumes a dollar of operating expense costs the same whether the vehicle is deadheading or carrying a rider. Is there a more defensible allocation using only public NTD fields (e.g., weighting by vehicle type or maintenance cost share), or is uniform attribution actually the right conservative default given what's publicly available?
6. **Zone-hour granularity may be the wrong resolution for the actual dispatch decision.** Every real-data model (AAR, and Chicago/MTA once trained) operates at hourly granularity. Real paratransit scheduling decisions happen at the individual-trip-request level with a same-day or next-day horizon. Is there a case for a finer-grained (15-30 min) model for near-term dispatch, with the hourly model reserved for capacity planning and the demand-curve/market-sizing story?
7. **The fictional reference agency (`business/reference_agency/`) remains necessary until the scheduler has a consented real booking stream.** Is a rideshare trip stream structurally close enough to paratransit bookings (subscription patterns, pooling eligibility, mobility constraints, and negotiation windows) to validate the scheduler, or should it be used only for geography while the booking metadata remains explicitly synthetic?

## Product and compliance positioning

8. **What evidence does an ADA grievance or compliance review actually require beyond a forecast explanation?** SHAP can describe which inputs influenced a demand forecast, but it cannot explain a scheduling decision. The product direction is a deterministic policy and solver log containing the feasible alternatives, failed constraints, human overrides, and outcome. The remaining question is which fields and retention/export conventions a compliance officer would require in practice.
9. **The self-serve savings calculator (business/ntd/savings_calculator.py) is public-data-only by design** (no PHI, no routing demo) specifically to stay clear of the locked "no public map demo" rule. Is a calculator that shows an agency's own NTD-derived savings number arguably still a public demo of the product's core value proposition in a way that rule was meant to prevent, or is the distinction (economics calculator vs. routing/scheduling demo) actually clean?
10. **Regulatory tailwind check:** S.2164 (Algorithmic Accountability Act of 2025) is real and pending in Senate Commerce as of mid-2026, with no paratransit-specific language. Is citing pending federal legislation as a "why now" in a YC application a credibility asset or a liability (reads as reaching, since it may never pass) — and is there a stronger, more concrete regulatory hook specific to ADA paratransit oversight that a domain-literate reviewer would know about that we're missing?
