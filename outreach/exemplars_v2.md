# Cold email exemplars — v3 (craft-corrected)

Supersedes v2. Rules and evidence in `craft_notes.md`; every figure computed by
`build_peer_insight.py` and re-derivable from `peer_insights.json` → `evidence`.

**What changed from v2, and why:**

| v2 | v3 | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "TCRTA vs the 171 operators your size" (7 words, Title Case, 36 chars) | `8th of 171` (3 words, lowercase, 10 chars) | lowercase beats Title Case **+21%**; 2–4 words ≈46% opens; ≤33 chars renders on mobile |
| 84–89 words | 55–70 words | 150+ words = **42% fewer replies** than <50; sub-80 is the 2026 sweet spot |
| "I build demand forecasting for paratransit. I'll show you where those hours sit." | observation carries it; no self-description | Braun: **ditch the pitch, poke the bear** — let them persuade themselves |
| "Worth 15 minutes?" | "Want it?" / "Curious?" | meeting asks trigger loss aversion; **interest-based CTAs ~30% success, 2× any other**; single ask lifts replies 35–42% |
| rank + median + dollar value all at once | hold part back | curiosity gap: provoke, don't satisfy — leave a reason to reply |

Kept deliberately: the word "free" in the body (the honest word, and the spam
risk is a myth — 1.7pts over baseline), the hedge on recoverable dollars, and
plain text with **zero links and no attachments** (the factors that actually
drive deliverability).

> Hi Derek,
>
> Of the 171 US operators running roughly TCRTA's demand-response volume, you

> 15% median.

>
> Want it?
>
> Arya

*62 words. Lever: save money. CTA: interest-based, single. Curiosity gap: "which
hours and which runs" is the thing they have to reply to get.*
