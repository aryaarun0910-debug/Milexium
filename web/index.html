01 / The story

Opaque software runs a statutory service.

ADA paratransit and Medicaid NEMT move millions of riders to dialysis, day programs and medical care. The dispatch systems behind them are black boxes.

/ 01

Decisions nobody can see into

Legacy schedulers place trips without showing their work. When a rider asks why their pickup moved by 40 minutes, the honest answer today is: the software decided. That erodes trust in every run it builds.

/ 02

Compliance assembled by hand

ADA rules and NTD filings demand a defensible record of every trip and every denial. Reconstructing that record from an opaque scheduler is days of manual work, every reporting cycle.

/ 03

Demand you cannot see coming

Dialysis runs Monday, Wednesday, Friday. Day programs load at nine. Weather cancels trips in waves. The pattern is right there in the booking history, but incumbent tools do not forecast it.

02 / The product

The audit trail is the product.

TransitPulse forecasts demand, schedules trips, and writes down why. Every assignment and every denial carries human-readable factors, the alternatives considered, and a structured reason code for each one rejected.

Audit log entry, synthetic trip data

Why this trip

Ask any trip in the schedule and get the full reasoning: which vehicles were considered, which constraint eliminated each alternative, and how the pickup landed inside the on-time window. Denials get the same treatment, with deterministic reason codes derived from the exact constraint that failed. This is the compliance trail, generated at decision time, not reconstructed after the fact.

Why this forecast

The demand model is explained the same way the scheduler is. Every zone-hour forecast decomposes into named factors a dispatcher already thinks in: what happened this slot last week, the trailing average, the day of week. Forecasts ship as calibrated ranges, not single numbers, so staffing decisions carry honest uncertainty.
