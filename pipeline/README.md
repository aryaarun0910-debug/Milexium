# TransitPulse pipeline (v1, synthetic)

Run in order:

```
py -3 pipeline/generate_trips.py    # synthetic county paratransit bookings, 12 months
py -3 pipeline/train_demand.py     # zone-hour XGBoost demand model, temporal validation
py -3 pipeline/schedule_trips.py   # one service day: pooling + assignment + audit trail
py -3 pipeline/make_demo_assets.py # renders docs/demo-assets/*.png
```

Current numbers (seed 42):

- Demand model: R2 0.839 vs 0.817 seasonal-naive, RMSE 0.434 vs 0.463, strict 90-day temporal holdout
- Scheduler: 229/253 requests placed, 77% in shared runs, 20 vehicles, deadhead 17% of drive time
- Every assignment and every denial in `output/audit_log.json` carries factors and rejected alternatives with reasons, plus a structured, deterministic reason `code` per rejected vehicle (`NO_ACCESSIBLE_CAPACITY`, `NEGOTIATION_WINDOW`, `SHIFT_END`) derived directly from the constraint that failed. This is the compliance trail; it is never generated by SHAP or any other model explanation. SHAP stays scoped to the demand forecast's "why this forecast" cards (see realdata/explanations_aar.json) — a genuinely separate concern from "why was this trip scheduled or denied," and the two must never be conflated.

## Honesty notes (say these out loud in any demo)

1. All data is synthetic. The generator is anchored to public NTD demand-response
   norms (trips/revenue-hour, subscription share, purpose mix, cancellation rates),
   but the model's headline R2 partly reflects that synthetic demand is more
   regular than real demand. The naive-baseline comparison is reported for
   exactly this reason. Replacing this with a real agency's trip data is the
   first pilot deliverable.
2. The scheduler is a v1 greedy insertion with hub pooling. No en-route
   interleaving, no re-optimization. Its job here is to demonstrate the audit
   trail, not to claim optimality.
3. The "suggested action" line on the capacity alert card is illustrative copy,
   not yet a computed recommendation.
4. Rider ids are synthetic. No PHI exists anywhere in this repo, and the real
   product must be built HIPAA-aware from day one for NEMT.
5. Real-data validation (realdata/) covers demand FORECASTING only, not
   dispatch DECISIONS. AAR is aggregate borough-hourly bookings, not
   individual-trip records — it can validate "how many bookings next hour,"
   not "which vehicle should take this specific rider." The scheduler above
   still runs on synthetic trip-level data because no individual-trip
   paratransit dataset is public. Real pilot data access, not more model
   sophistication, is the actual unlock for validating dispatch decisions.
   Concretely: docs/demo-assets/why_this_forecast_real.png and
   demand_curve_real.png (realdata/make_real_demo_assets.py) are the real
   equivalents of two of this pipeline's five demo assets. The other three
   (why_this_trip, capacity_alert, fleet_timeline) stay synthetic until a
   real trip-level booking stream exists — they are not fakeable honestly
   with aggregate data.
